Is the Geumsan Wellness Walking Challenge a Difficult Hike?

A personal review of the Geumsan Wellness Walking Challenge, including its uphill section, event experience, reservation notes, and accessibility considerations.

Geumsan, Chungcheongnam-do, South Korea

  • #Geumsan
  • #WellnessWalking
  • #KoreaTravel

Based on my experience, the Geumsan Wellness Walking Challenge was easy to moderate rather than a flat walk. The route included a short climb on Mount Geumsan, so it may suit visitors who are comfortable with some uphill walking, but it may not be ideal if you strongly dislike climbing.

I joined the challenge during the Geumsan Ginseng Festival in Geumsan, Chungcheongnam-do, South Korea, on October 3, 2026. This is a personal account of the event I attended; check the organizer’s current announcement for future dates and route details.

Quick Facts

  • Location: Geumsan, Chungcheongnam-do, South Korea
  • Date I attended: October 3, 2026
  • Activity: A wellness walk with a short uphill section on Mount Geumsan
  • Difficulty in my experience: Easy to moderate
  • Reservation: Traveller’s Club, a Korean-language website
  • Event format: A one-day event

What the Walk Was Like

Although the event was called a walking challenge, part of the route went uphill on Mount Geumsan. I would describe the difficulty as easy to moderate. That is my personal impression, not an official difficulty rating, and the climb could feel harder if you are not used to walking uphill.

The challenge took place as a one-day event. Participants received items such as water, snacks, and candy. There was also a lucky draw, where one participant could win a surprise gift.

How to Reserve If You Do Not Speak Korean

I reserved my place through Traveller’s Club, a Korean-language website. If you are not comfortable reading Korean, you may need help translating the reservation page. Consider asking a Korean-speaking friend to help before booking, and confirm the event details with the organizer.

Woodi and Gadi: A Playful Chungcheongnam-do Mascot Moment

The event featured Woodi, a character representing Chungcheongnam-do, holding a smaller character named Gadi. Saying “Woodi Gadi” aloud sounds similar to a Korean phrase meaning “Where should I go?” That wordplay made the characters a memorable part of my visit.

Is This Challenge Right for You?

Consider joining if you enjoy festival activities and are comfortable with a short uphill walk. If you need an accessible or mostly flat route, ask the organizer about the current course before making a reservation. The event I attended was held for one day, so check the latest announcement to find out whether it will be offered again.
