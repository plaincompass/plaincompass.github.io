No, it is not that difficult to hike. The real problem is transportation: there is no public transport to the area.

Based on my experience, the Geumsan Wellness Walking Challenge was easy to moderate rather than a flat walk. The route included a short uphill section on Mount Geumsan, and the climb was somewhat steep, so it may suit visitors who are comfortable with some uphill walking, but it may not be ideal if you strongly dislike climbing.

I joined the challenge during the Geumsan Ginseng Festival in Geumsan, Chungcheongnam-do, South Korea, on October 3, 2026. This is my personal account of the event I attended; check the organizer’s current announcement for future dates and route details.

What the walk was like

Although the event was called a walking challenge, part of the route went uphill on Mount Geumsan. I would describe the difficulty as easy to moderate, but the uphill section was a bit steeper than I expected. That is my personal impression, not an official difficulty rating, and the climb may feel harder if you are not used to walking uphill.

The challenge took place as a one-day event. Participants received items such as water, snacks, and candy. There was also a lucky draw, where one participant could win a surprise gift.

One thing I especially liked was that the walk could also include a visit to the Seven Hundred Patriotic Martyrs Tomb (Geumsan). This is a historic memorial site dedicated to 700 patriots, and it gave the area a deeper cultural and historical atmosphere. It was a nice bonus during the trip.

How to reserve if you do not speak Korean

I reserved my spot through Traveller’s Club, a Korean-language website. If you are not comfortable reading Korean, you may need help translating the reservation page. Consider asking a Korean-speaking friend to help before booking, and confirm the event details with the organizer.

Woodi and Gadi: a playful Chungcheongnam-do mascot moment

The event featured Woodi, a character representing Chungcheongnam-do, holding a smaller character named Gadi. Saying “Woodi Gadi” aloud sounds similar to a Korean phrase meaning “Where should I go?” That wordplay made the characters a memorable part of my visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this challenge right for you?

Consider joining if you enjoy festival activities and are comfortable with a short uphill walk. If you need an accessible or mostly flat route, ask the organizer about the current course before making a reservation. The event I attended was held for one day, so check the latest announcement to see whether it will be offered again.

Geumsan Wellness Challenge in 2026: Are there prizes?

Yes. They gave us snacks and water for hiking, along with a local-use voucher worth 5,000 KRW that could be used at Geumsan Market, as well as a ginseng alcohol drink.

Is the market near Geumsan?

If you are asking how to reach it by transport, yes. But not on foot. It takes about 10 minutes by car from Geumsan to the market.

What can I buy at the market?

Geumsan is known for its high-quality ginseng. You can buy fresh ginseng, which costs 2,500 KRW per piece or 10,000 KRW for four pieces. All the shops in the market seemed to have the same price.

Are there any other prizes at the festival challenge?

Yes. They also held a lucky draw, and the prizes included ginseng drinks, a Gadi doll (keyring), and shopper bags.

Was it fun? Are you going to join again next year?

Yes, it was fun. And getting prizes made it even more enjoyable.
