Is Mt. Gwanak a Good Mountain to Hike During a Seoul Trip?

A personal hiking guide to Mt. Gwanak from Gwacheon Station, including the route to Yeonjuam Temple, trail tips, and a quiet prayer spot near the summit.

Yes. Mt. Gwanak can be a rewarding hike during a Seoul trip if you want a mountain trail that is reachable by subway and are prepared for a strenuous uphill walk. The Gwacheon Station route has rocky sections and many stairs, so I would not describe it as an easy stroll. On my hike, it took me about two and a half hours to reach the top.

Who should hike Mt. Gwanak?

Good for

  • Travelers staying in Seoul who want to reach a mountain trail by subway.
  • Hikers who are comfortable with a sustained uphill walk, rocky ground, and stairs.
  • Visitors who enjoy city views and a rest stop at Yeonjuam Temple.

Less suitable for

  • Visitors looking for a flat, easy walk.
  • Hikers who have difficulty with steep stairs or uneven, rocky ground.
  • Anyone who is uncomfortable with the exposed rocky section near the prayer area.

Consider your fitness, mobility, weather, and trail conditions before choosing this route.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The trailhead is reachable by Seoul Subway Line 4.
  • The route passes Yeonjuam Temple, where I found restrooms and food vendors during my visit.
  • The uphill route offers views, including a viewpoint near the temple.

Cons

  • The ascent includes rocky sections, steep uphill walking, and many stairs.
  • The route can be crowded, especially around narrow sections and photo stops.
  • The rocky approach to the prayer area felt hazardous to me and requires extra care.

How to get to the trail from Gwacheon Station

  1. Take Subway Line 4 to Gwacheon Station and use Exit 7.
  2. From the exit, go straight along the large road.
  3. At the bridge, turn left and continue uphill until you reach Gwacheon Middle School.
  4. In front of the middle school, cross the road and go uphill toward Gwacheon HyangGuo. This is the start of Trail 2 on Mt. Gwanak.
  5. Continue uphill on the wider road. In my experience, the route was straightforward to follow.
  6. After about 40 minutes of uphill walking, you should reach Yeonjuam Temple. There are public restrooms there, and when I visited, small vendors sold cup noodles and ice cream.
  7. Continue uphill on the narrower path behind the shop. The route has many stairs and can be busy, so take care and give other hikers room.
  8. Near the top, I reached a large rock where hikers stopped for photos. Wait your turn and be careful on the uphill section.

Difficulty, time, and what to bring

The route was moderately difficult for me, not an easy city walk. Parts of the trail are rocky, and the long stair sections make sturdy hiking shoes a good idea. Trekking poles may help, but I found them optional. Bring water.

The information page I checked estimated about two hours for the ascent. It took me two and a half hours to reach the top, so allow extra time for your pace, breaks, and conditions. I did not record a round-trip time, so plan your return separately.

Yeonjuam Temple is a useful rest stop on this route. During my visit, it had public restrooms and vendors selling cup noodles for about 3,000 won and ice cream for about 2,000 won; they accepted cash only. These were my observations at the time and may no longer be current, so bring your own water and some cash rather than relying on the vendors.

The prayer place near the summit

Some people describe Mt. Gwanak as having “fire energy” and visit a small prayer area near the summit. This is a local belief, not a promise that wishes will come true. I first heard about the belief through online discussions and a Korean fortune teller’s video.

To reach the prayer area I visited, I climbed onto a large rock and followed a narrow path beside it to a small set of steps leading down. The rocky section felt hazardous to me; take care and do not attempt it if conditions or your footing feel unsafe. The prayer area has a Buddha statue. Please be quiet and avoid taking photos with sound or flash out of respect for people praying.

I prayed there, and about a month later I got a job at a company I was excited to join. I cannot say the prayer caused it, but the timing made the experience meaningful to me.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mt. Gwanak suitable for beginner hikers?

The route in this guide has rocky sections, steep uphill walking, and many stairs. I found it moderately difficult rather than an easy walk. Beginners should consider their fitness and comfort with uneven trails before choosing this route.

How long does it take to hike Mt. Gwanak from Gwacheon Station?

The information page I checked estimated about two hours for the ascent, while it took me two and a half hours to reach the top. These are ascent times, not round-trip times; your pace and breaks will affect the total.

How can I get to the Mt. Gwanak trail by public transport?

Take Seoul Subway Line 4 to Gwacheon Station and use Exit 7. From there, follow the directions in this guide toward Gwacheon Middle School and Trail 2.

What should I bring for the Gwacheon Station route?

Bring water and wear sturdy hiking shoes for the rocky sections. Trekking poles are optional. I also recommend carrying cash if you plan to buy food at Yeonjuam Temple, although vendor availability can change.

Is there a cable car on the route described here?

This guide describes a hiking route from Gwacheon Station and does not use or verify a cable car. Check current local visitor information if you need to confirm available transport options.

Can international visitors hike Mt. Gwanak?

This guide covers a public-transport route and personal hiking experience, but does not verify current access rules or trail closures. Check local notices before setting out, especially if you need current information in another language.
