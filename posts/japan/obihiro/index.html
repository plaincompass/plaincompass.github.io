Obihiro Winter Getaway: Quiet Hokkaido with Buta-don

A quiet winter trip to Obihiro, Hokkaido, featuring delicious Buta-don, hot springs, and peaceful winter scenery, with easy direct flight access but less snow than expected.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Much less crowded than major Hokkaido destinations such as Sapporo or Biei.
  • Delicious Buta‑don that was the highlight of the trip.
  • Hot springs and peaceful winter scenery.
  • Wide plains around Obihiro provide impressive views of snow-covered mountain ranges.
  • Convenient direct flight made the trip straightforward.

Cons

  • Snow volume was lower than expected, limiting winter activities.
  • Not ideal for those seeking deep snow or high‑altitude adventures.

Arrival and first impressions

Flying into Obihiro felt surprisingly easy, thanks to a direct flight that cut through the usual congestion of larger airports. The city greeted me with a calm, almost quiet atmosphere that was a pleasant contrast to the bustle I’d experienced in Sapporo. As soon as I stepped onto the airport’s cold, clean tarmac, I could feel the winter air already settling in, and the city’s modest scale promised a slower pace of life.

Food highlight: Buta‑don

The moment I tasted the Buta‑don, I knew I was in for something special. The pork was tender, the sauce rich and savory, and every bite seemed to capture the essence of Hokkaido’s winter bounty. It was the highlight of my trip, and I found myself savoring each spoonful with a sense of gratitude for the local culinary tradition.

Hot springs experience

After a day of wandering the quiet streets, I headed to a nearby hot spring. The water was warm and inviting, a soothing contrast to the frosty air outside. I spent a generous amount of time soaking, letting the steam rise around me while the quiet of the countryside wrapped me in a sense of calm. It was the kind of relaxed, restorative experience that only a truly tranquil place can offer.

Winter scenery and Nukabira Lake

One of the most memorable moments was visiting Nukabira Lake. During my visit, the lake was covered in snow and surrounded by trees, creating a quiet winter landscape that felt completely different from the scenery around Obihiro itself.

The area around the lake felt calm and isolated, making it one of my favorite places for photography. The combination of the frozen lake, snow-covered ground, and surrounding forest created a distinctly winter atmosphere.

The scenery around Obihiro was very different. Much of the region consists of wide open plains, and from those plains I could see snow-covered mountain ranges stretching across the horizon. The contrast between the flat landscape and the distant white mountains created some of the most impressive views of the trip.

I also enjoyed photographing views of Mt. Asahidake from a distance. Although I could not climb it because of weather and access conditions, the mountain remained one of the most striking features of the winter scenery.

Packing and gear

I came prepared with warm winter clothing, hand warmers, winter shoes, and a camera. The gear served me well: the warm layers kept me comfortable in the chilly air, hand warmers added a practical touch for those cold moments, and the winter shoes provided traction on the slick surfaces. The camera captured the quiet beauty of the landscape, and I found myself looking back at the photos with a sense of satisfaction.

Crowd level

The city’s quiet atmosphere was a deliberate choice, and it paid off. Obihiro was noticeably less crowded than the major Hokkaido destinations I had visited before. This lower crowd level made it easy to enjoy the local sights, food, and hot springs without the bustle that can accompany larger tourist spots.

Final thoughts

If you prioritize food, hot springs, and a relaxed atmosphere over major sightseeing attractions, Obihiro is a solid winter destination. The city’s quieter vibe, delicious Buta‑don, and peaceful winter scenery create an experience that is both comforting and memorable. Just be prepared for less snow and the possibility of having to adjust winter activity plans.
