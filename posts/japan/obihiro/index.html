Obihiro, Hokkaido: Snowy Plains, Red Squirrels, and the Best Butadon

Direct flight from Seoul opens Obihiro. Explore snowy Tokachigawasuikei Park, spot red squirrels, and enjoy the best Butadon in Japan.

Obihiro, Japan

I flew directly from Seoul to Obihiro in February 2026 on a three‑hour AeroK flight that landed at 4 PM. From the airport I took a 1,000‑yen shuttle to Obihiro Station and checked into Comfort Hotel Obihiro just outside the station.

The highlight was Tokachigawasuikei Ryokuchi Park, where snowy plains and scattered trees gave a Nordic feel. I walked slowly with crampons on the slightly slippery paths, and on the way back from the park to the hotel, I encountered a red squirrel, which let me feel the nature of Obihiro. I savored the best Butadon I’ve had in Japan along with local dango, takoyaki, and the Hokkaido‑only Sapporo Classic. Cards were accepted everywhere, and the total trip duration was 3 nights and 4 days, with Obihiro visited on the first and last days.

Why I Visited

I had just learned that a direct flight from Seoul to Obihiro had opened in February 2026, and I was eager to explore another small city in Hokkaido after my trip to Sapporo. The new flight schedule made it convenient to reach a place that hadn’t been on my radar before.

What Stood Out

The first thing that struck me was the snowy mountains standing like a folding screen against the vast plains. I stayed at a hotel near Obihiro Station and walked to Tokachigawasuikei Ryokuchi Park. From the park, I could see the whole landscape in a blanket of white. The trees scattered around created an exotic, almost Nordic, atmosphere. I walked slowly through the park, feeling the serenity of the first footprints in the white snow and even met a red squirrel.

Food was another memorable part of the visit. The Butadon at a local spot was the best I’ve tasted in Japan, and even the dango and takoyaki from the supermarket were delicious. I also tried the Sapporo Classic, which is sold only within Hokkaido. The combination of snowy scenery and tasty food made the experience special.

Practical Notes

  • Flight: A direct AeroK flight from Seoul to Obihiro lasted about three hours. The service is seasonal, which is the only downside I noted.
  • Arrival: I landed at 4 PM and took a shuttle from the airport to Obihiro Station for 1,000 yen.
  • Accommodation: I stayed at Comfort Hotel Obihiro, located just outside the station.
  • Walking: The paths near the park were slightly slippery, so I brought crampons and walked with caution.
  • Weather: Clear skies, but roads were a bit slick.
  • Language: I had almost no language skills, but it didn’t hinder anything.
  • Payments: Cards were accepted at almost all places, and there was no waiting time for transactions.
  • Crowd: Obihiro Station itself was crowded during my arrival.
  • Duration: I spent one night on the first day and a half‑day on the last day, departing at 12 PM.
  • Difficulty: Rated 2 on a scale where 5 is hardest; the walk was manageable with the right precautions.

Final Thoughts

Strolling through Tokachigawasuikei Ryokuchi Park, surrounded by snow‑covered plains and trees, was soothing for the mind. The photos I took—snow, trees, and the expansive landscape—captured a Nordic vibe that I hadn’t expected in Hokkaido. The food, especially the Butadon, was the most delicious I’ve experienced in Japan; it’s clear why this place is considered the birthplace of that dish. The Butadon shop inside Obihiro Station was constantly filled with people, and the owner was constantly grilling meat over charcoal, adding to the authentic feel.
