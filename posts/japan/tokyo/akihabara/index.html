Akihabara Hunt: PlayStation Portal, Crepes & Crowded Streets

Spent 2 hours in crowded Akihabara searching for a PlayStation Portal. No luck, but enjoyed crepes and takoyaki near Bic Camera.

Akihabara, Tokyo

I visited Akihabara in April 2024 hoping to snag a PlayStation Portal. The district’s reputation for gaming and anime made it the obvious choice, and I had heard that Sony’s headquarters might still have a few units left. Although online reports suggested it was difficult to buy in Japan, I thought that if I put in the effort, I might find one.

The main street was packed with people and the atmosphere lively. I walked about 20 minutes from a hotel near Tokyo Station, using the subway and then walking. I checked Sony’s headquarters and a second‑hand store but found no Portal in stock. After the hunt, I enjoyed crepes and takoyaki near Akihabara Bic Camera, which was a tasty break from the crowded streets.

Why I Visited

I went to Akihabara in April 2024 to try to buy a PlayStation Portal. The city’s reputation for gaming and anime merchandise made it the obvious destination, and I had heard that Sony’s headquarters might still have a few units left.

What Stood Out

The main street was packed with people—very crowded, and the atmosphere was lively. The shops were densely packed, especially those popular with anime fans from around the world. I walked for about 20 minutes from a hotel near Tokyo Station, taking the subway and then walking. The walk itself was easy and required no significant effort.

While searching for the PlayStation Portal, I stopped at several places, including Sony’s headquarters and a second‑hand store, but none had the item in stock. After the search, I ate crepes and takoyaki near Akihabara Bic Camera. The food was delicious, and it was a pleasant break after the hunt.

Practical Notes

  • Transport: From the hotel, I took the subway and walked.
  • Timing: I arrived at 10 a.m. and spent about 2 hours exploring.
  • Crowd: Very crowded; the streets were busy.
  • Language: Store staff could speak a little English, so communication was somewhat possible.
  • Payment: Cards are accepted.
  • Waiting time: None reported.
  • Fitness: No significant effort required.
  • Photo opportunity: Not ideal for a main shopping photo, but a backdrop of many people could produce a distinctive shot.

Final Thoughts

Akihabara was a lively street filled with amazing figurines and anime culture. Despite the excitement and the many shopping streets, I couldn’t find a PlayStation Portal—likely sold out by April 2024. I was disappointed because I wanted a Ghost of Tsushima CD, but only the Japanese version was available and I couldn’t purchase it due to language barriers. Nevertheless, the day was enjoyable, and the crepes and takoyaki were a delicious highlight.
