Cycling Lake Kawaguchiko: Electric Bike Picnic & Mt. Fuji Views

Rented an electric bike to cycle steep paths around Lake Kawaguchiko. Skipped crowded Lawson spots for a quiet picnic with clear Mt. Fuji views.

Lake Kawaguchiko

Public transport access
★★★★★ Excellent (5/5)

I decided to cycle around Lake Kawaguchiko to see Mt. Fuji in person after seeing so many photos. I took a highway bus from Tokyo Station, arriving at the lake terminal at 9 am, then went straight to 81cafe to rent an electric bike. The shop opened at 9:30 am, so I waited 20 minutes and paid 3,200 yen for a full‑day rental, which made the steep sections of the path manageable.

The ride lasted about five hours, stopping at several spots where Fuji framed the scenery. I found a quiet spot in the lake park, had a convenience‑store lunch, and watched the mountain rise above the water. The town was crowded, but the lake area was peaceful. However, the spring pollen allergy was physically taxing. If you visit this lake in spring, be sure to get a prescription for pollen allergy medicine. The terrain had some steep parts and was very wide, so an electric bike was essential overall. Instead of restaurants with long waits, I bought a convenience-store lunch for a picnic, which was the most memorable experience. I was able to take many beautiful photos, enjoy the food while fully appreciating Mt. Fuji, and relax by the peaceful riverside.

Why I Visited

I had seen countless photos of Lake Kawaguchiko with Mt. Fuji in the background and wanted to experience that view myself. The idea of cycling around the lake while looking at the mountain was especially appealing.

What Stood Out

I was worried that Mt. Fuji would not be visible if the weather was even slightly cloudy, but the day I visited was exceptionally clear. Checking the real‑time CCTV before leaving Tokyo, I was relieved to see that the mountain would be visible. Arriving at 9:00 am, I went to 81cafe (Rental Cycle) and waited 20 minutes to rent an electric bike; the shop opened at 9:30 am, so getting there early was a good idea. The daily rental cost was 3,200 yen, a bit pricey but worth it because the electric bike made the wide, steep sections of the lake’s path easy to handle. In the end, I didn't feel the money was wasted at all.

I rode around the lake for about five hours, stopping at several spots where Mt. Fuji framed the scenery. The most memorable moment was laying a blanket in the lake park, eating a convenience‑store lunch, and watching Mt. Fuji. The reflection of Mt. Fuji in the flowing water was also beautiful. Especially at the time of my visit, there was still snow on the peak of Mt. Fuji, allowing me to fully enjoy the Mt. Fuji seen in photos.

Lake Kawaguchiko is famous for photo spots in front of the Lawson convenience store, but in reality, it was extremely crowded with many people taking photos, and it looked very dangerous due to cars passing by. Since every place in Kawaguchiko has Mt. Fuji as a background and can be a photo spot, I recommend not forcing a photo shoot in front of Lawson.

Practical Notes

  • Transport: Took a highway bus from Tokyo Station to Kawaguchiko Terminal (≈1 h 40 min).
  • Arrival: 9:00 am.
  • Bike Rental: 81cafe (Rental Cycle) – open 9:30 am, full‑day rental only, 3,200 yen. Arrived at 9:10 am, waited 20 minutes.
  • Crowds: The town of Kawaguchiko was extremely crowded; after passing the town, the lake area became quieter.
  • Allergies: Bring allergy medicine, especially for pollen in spring.
  • Food: All restaurants had long queues; a convenience‑store lunch and picnic were practical.
  • Payment: Most places accepted cards.
  • Fitness: No effort needed with the electric bike; a regular bike would be exhausting on the steep sections.

Final Thoughts

Lake Kawaguchiko was truly beautiful. Once you rent a bike and enter the lake area, the bustle of the town fades, revealing a quiet and peaceful lakeside where you can enjoy the view of Mt. Fuji. I recommend not forcing photo shoots on the road in dangerous areas like in front of the Lawson convenience store within Kawaguchiko town. Renting an electric bike is essential; renting a regular bike would be a significant physical burden. The middle sections of the path are steep and the area is very wide.

In my case, buying a convenience-store lunch and having a picnic in a quiet place was the most memorable. Don't force yourself to wait in long lines at cafes or restaurants; instead, buy from a convenience store and find a quiet spot anywhere to fully feel Mt. Fuji and the lake. You will feel peaceful. Also, in spring, there is more pollen than you might think. Even if you don't usually have allergy symptoms, be sure to bring pollen allergy medicine. If allergy symptoms appear, it becomes difficult to enjoy the trip physically.
