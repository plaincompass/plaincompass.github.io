Tokachikawa Onsen (Obihiro): A Peaceful Hot Spring Escape in Hokkaido

The Hot Spring Experience

I traveled to Tokachikawa Onsen in Obihiro, Hokkaido, in February 2026, specifically to enjoy the hot spring water that emerges from moss. This is a unique feature of the region, described as the only kind in Hokkaido.

My best memory of the trip was soaking in the open-air bath at the hotel. I went down around 6:00 AM to the open-air bath inside the hotel. Since it was dawn, I was the only one there. I was able to enjoy the hot spring all by myself. The air was cold, and my body was warm. This contrast made me feel incredibly happy and truly relaxed. The solitude of the early morning bath was the highlight of the stay.

Accommodation and Food

I stayed at Hotel Tetra Resort Tokachigawa for a one-night, two-day trip. I booked a dinner and breakfast package at the hotel, and the food was quite good.

One of the nice touches was the "Milk Bar," which offers free Hokkaido milk. It was perfect to drink after a bath. The overall atmosphere of the stay was peaceful and enjoyable.

Transportation and Logistics

Getting to and from the area requires some planning, especially regarding payment and transfers.

I traveled by city bus from Nukabira Lake to Tokachikawa Onsen, which took 1 hour 30 minutes. The next day, I took a city bus to Obihiro Station, which took 30 minutes.

There is a shuttle between Obihiro Station and the Tokachikawa Onsen village. Guests staying at partner hotels get free bus fare, so it is good to keep this in mind. The hotel I stayed at was not a partner hotel, so I did not receive the free fare.

If you are coming from Nukabira Lake, you will need to make one transfer. A transfer is required at the Kinohokyo (Kinonohokyo) stop.

Payment methods vary by mode of transport. They accepted cards in most cases for other services. However, buses only accept cash, so please be careful and bring enough cash for bus fare.

What to Bring

Since there are no convenience stores or supermarkets near the hotel, it is essential to bring your own supplies.

  • Cash: For bus fare, as cards are not accepted on buses.
  • Snacks and other items: Anything you would typically buy at a supermarket, as none are available nearby.
  • Sapporo Classic: A beer you can only taste in Hokkaido, known for its fresh taste.

Photography Spots

If you walk about 20 minutes from the hotel, you will find the Onsen Tourism Center at 14 Chome-1-1 Tokachigawaonsenkita, Otofuke, Kato District, Hokkaido 080-0262 Japan. There is an Idenoyu no Iwasaka there, where you can view the rocks and take photos.

Weather and Expectations

The conditions were clear. I thought Hokkaido in February would have a lot of snow, but there was less snow compared to Sapporo when I visited in March last year.

Final thoughts

Overall, it was a very peaceful and enjoyable trip. The absence of crowds and the quiet nature of the small town added to the relaxation. While the lack of nearby shopping or convenience stores was a downside, it was manageable if you prepared by bringing snacks and cash. The combination of the unique moss-emerging hot spring, the good food at the hotel, and the free milk bar made for a satisfying experience. It is a destination best suited for those seeking leisure and quiet rather than infrastructure or intense activities.
