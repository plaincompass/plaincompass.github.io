Walking on the Sky: My Journey to Chaka Salt Lake

Standing in the center of an endless sheet of crystalline white, where the horizon dissolves and heaven merges with the earth, is an experience that stays with you long after the dried salt crust has washed off your boots. For years, I had dreamed of standing across a vast salt flat, watching pure clouds duplicate beneath my feet. While Bolivia’s Salar de Uyuni often takes the global crown, China's Qinghai Province boasts its own legendary "Mirror of the Sky"—Chaka Salt Lake.

In August 2025, I finally packed my camera gear, braced myself for high-altitude conditions, and set off into the Tibetan Plateau to see if Chaka lived up to the digital hype or if it was merely an overcrowded tourist hotspot.

Here is my honest, boots-on-the-ground review of traveling to Chaka Salt Lake, complete with practical transit advice, photography secrets, and the realities of navigating this surreal high-altitude landscape.

The Journey: Overnight Rails Across the Plateau

Getting to Chaka is half the adventure. Located over 3,000 meters above sea level and roughly 300 kilometers west of Xining (the capital of Qinghai Province), it requires a conscious travel commitment.

My favorite part of the entire trip was opting for the seasonal sleeper train from Xining straight to Chaka. Boarding in the evening, drifting off to the rhythmic clack of the tracks, and waking up as the rugged Tibetan alpine meadows gave way to glistening white basins was pure romance. Traveling overnight turned transit time into an immersive experience rather than a chore.

However, the railway schedule has a massive catch: there is currently only one designated tourist train per day between Xining and Chaka Salt Lake, and only one return train.

This single-train bottleneck creates a logistical headache. If you miss that departure or fail to secure a sleeper ticket weeks in advance during the peak summer months, your only alternatives are long-distance private hire cars or multi-leg public buses that swallow entire days. If you plan to take the train, lock down your tickets the moment your dates are finalized, and build padding into your schedule.

The Reality on the Ground: Altitude, Glare, and Swarms

Chaka Salt Lake is undeniably popular. Arriving in August—the height of China’s domestic travel season—meant the primary entrance was buzzing with tour groups, red-dressed travelers queuing for mirror-reflection poses, and loudspeakers.

On a subjective scale, I would rate the physical difficulty of this trip around 4 out of 5. The challenge isn't intense hiking; it's the environment:

  • The lake sits at an elevation of approximately 3,059 meters (over 10,000 feet). Even gentle walking on the lake bed can leave you winded quickly.
  • The sun is relentless. Sunlight bounces off the white salt crust from every conceivable angle, multiplying UV exposure.
  • The sheer density of people near the central historic scenic area (Area 1) can be overwhelming if you crave peaceful, solitary nature.

If you stay near the main entrance and the old narrow-gauge train tracks of Area 1, you will likely spend more time waiting for people to step out of your frame than enjoying the landscape.

The Secret to Great Photos: Head to Sky One Scenic Area

If you want the ethereal, solitary photos you see in travel brochures, you have to bypass the main crush of Area 1.

Take a taxi and head over to the newer Sky One Scenic Area (Chaka Yikong). Most large tour buses dump their passengers in the historic zone, leaving Sky One significantly less congested.

Here, the infrastructure is newer, the monumental salt sculptures carved directly out of the mineral deposits are genuinely awe-inspiring, and the open lake flats stretch out with far fewer human interruptions.

Pro-Tip for Photographers: Look into booking a stay at the Chaka Sky One Hotel. Guests staying on-site often have access to ticket packages allowing early entry before regular opening gates open to the general public. Walking out onto the glassy water at dawn when the surface is dead calm, the air is crisp, and not another soul is in sight makes the entire journey worthwhile.

Photography Tips for Chaka Salt Lake

  • Watch the Wind: A true mirror reflection requires undisturbed water. Mornings generally offer calmer breezes than late afternoons. If a gust picks up, the lake surface ripples, and the reflection vanishes.
  • Bring a Polarizer and ND Filters: The glare off the salt is blinding. A circular polarizing filter (CPL) helps manage reflections, while neutral density (ND) filters allow you to capture smooth long exposures of the distant mountains.
  • Color Contrast: Because the earth is blinding white and the sky is deep cobalt, wearing contrasting colors (crimson, emerald, or deep ochre) creates a striking silhouette against the landscape.
  • Stay for the Night Sky: Do not pack up when the sun dips. Chaka sits far away from heavy industrial light pollution. On a clear night, the Tibetan sky erupts into an unbelievable blanket of stars, with the Milky Way visible to the naked eye.

Pros and Cons of Visiting Chaka Salt Lake

What I Loved

  • The sleeper train journey from Xining offers an unforgettable, classic rail adventure.
  • The optical illusions and mirror reflections across the salt flats are breathtaking when conditions align.
  • Sky One Scenic Area provides expansive, quieter grounds and remarkable salt art.
  • Incredible opportunities for astrophotography once the day-trippers disperse.

What Could Be Better

  • Inflexible rail transport, with only one direct train connection per day each way.
  • Summer crowds can be intense, especially around Area 1 and the vintage train tracks.
  • High altitude combined with intense solar glare can quickly trigger headaches or exhaustion.
  • Commercialized photo setups and rental red shoe covers can detract from the wild, natural aesthetic.

What to Pack

Do not visit unprepared. The combination of plateau weather and reflective salt can be punishing without the right supplies:

  • Oxygen Canisters & Altitude Medication: Buy portable oxygen cans in Xining before heading up. If you are prone to altitude sickness, start taking your medication a day or two prior.
  • Polarized Sunglasses: Non-negotiable. Without UV-rated polarized lenses, you will spend the entire day squinting against searing glare.
  • High-SPF Sunscreen & Lip Balm: Apply and reapply constantly. The reflection burns under your chin and nose just as easily as on your forehead.
  • Waterproof Boot Covers: Salt brine will permanently crust and damage regular leather or mesh sneakers. Rent the plastic overshoes on-site or bring your own sturdy waterproof sandals.
  • Plenty of Water: Plateau air is bone-dry, and breathing at altitude dehydrates you twice as fast.

Who Should Go—And Who Should Skip It

This trip is ideal for:

  • Landscape photographers looking for minimalist, surreal compositions.
  • Travelers who want the experience of walking across a vast mirror flat without journeying all the way to South America.
  • Independent travelers who love iconic train journeys across open frontiers.

You might want to skip this trip if:

  • You have already explored the massive, pristine expanse of Salar de Uyuni in Bolivia (Chaka is much smaller and far more commercialized).
  • You cannot tolerate heavy crowds or commercialized scenic parks.
  • You are highly sensitive to high-altitude sickness or harsh solar environments.

Final Thoughts

Chaka Salt Lake demands patience. Between securing limited train tickets, adjusting to the thin air, and navigating tour crowds, it is not a casual day trip.

To truly appreciate it, allow at least three nights and four days for the broader loop originating from Xining. Give yourself enough time to acclimatize, visit Qinghai Lake along the way, escape to Sky One Scenic Area, and wait for the perfect pocket of calm morning water. When the wind stops and the clouds float effortlessly above and beneath you, the logistical effort disappears into thin air.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get to Chaka Salt Lake from Xining?

You can take the daily direct tourist train from Xining Station directly to Chaka Station, which takes about four to five hours. Alternatively, you can hire a private driver or take a long-distance bus from Xining, typically combining the trip with a stop at Qinghai Lake.

What is the best month to visit Chaka Salt Lake?

July and August offer the warmest weather and best water levels for mirror reflections, though it is also peak domestic tourist season. June and September offer pleasant weather with fewer crowds.

What should I do to prevent altitude sickness at Chaka Salt Lake?

Chaka Salt Lake sits above 3,000 meters. Spend a day acclimatizing in Xining first, stay hydrated, avoid strenuous running or jumping on the salt flats, and carry portable oxygen canisters and altitude sickness medication.

Why should I choose Sky One Scenic Area over Area 1?

Sky One Scenic Area is newer, spacious, and features massive salt sculptures. Most large tour groups gather at Area 1, making Sky One significantly less crowded and far easier for clean photography.

How many days do you need for a trip to Chaka Salt Lake?

It is best to plan at least three nights and four days starting from Xining. This allows sufficient time to travel, acclimatize, photograph the lake during calm morning hours, and see nearby sights like Qinghai Lake.

Is Chaka Salt Lake worth visiting if I have already been to Salar de Uyuni?

If you have already experienced the vastness and remoteness of Salar de Uyuni in Bolivia, Chaka Salt Lake may feel small and commercialized. However, it offers unique cultural scenery and Tibetan plateau landscapes that provide a different atmosphere.

Can you walk directly on the salt lake?

Yes, visitors are permitted to walk into designated shallow areas of the lake. Wearing protective shoe covers or waterproof boots is strongly recommended to protect your skin and footwear from the harsh brine.
