Is the Datong Volcano Viewpoint Worth Visiting Without a Tour?

A personal walking guide to the Datong Volcano area, including the volcano viewpoint near the volcano district, trail conditions, volcano views, and practical travel tips.

Yes. If you are already staying near the Datong Volcano area, I think the volcano viewpoint is worth visiting. The route is easy to follow, the walking is not difficult, and the viewpoint offers wide views of several extinct volcanoes rising from a flat plain. While the main tourist section of the geopark may require a taxi, the viewpoint near the volcano district can be reached on foot.

Who should visit the Datong Volcano Viewpoint?

Good for

  • Travelers staying near the volcano district.
  • Visitors who enjoy easy walks and viewpoints.
  • Photographers looking for unusual landscapes.
  • People interested in extinct volcanoes.

Less suitable for

  • Travelers expecting a highly developed tourist attraction.
  • Visitors looking for challenging hiking trails.
  • Anyone sensitive to strong sunlight.
  • Travelers hoping to reach every volcano without transportation.

Consider weather conditions and visibility before visiting.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Easy to reach on foot from nearby accommodation.
  • Well-maintained walking paths.
  • Unique volcanic scenery.
  • Very few crowds when I visited.

Cons

  • Almost no natural shade.
  • Air pollution may reduce visibility.
  • The most famous volcanoes are farther away.
  • Public transportation in the area is limited.

How to get to the volcano viewpoint

  1. Start from Datong Kaijie Inn (Volcano Park Branch).
  2. Follow the paved road uphill toward the volcano park.
  3. Continue straight until reaching the park entrance.
  4. Enter the park and climb the stair sections.
  5. Continue along the maintained walking paths.
  6. Follow signs and paths toward the viewpoint area.
  7. From the viewpoint, additional walking routes continue toward nearby volcanoes.

I found the route straightforward and easy to follow.

Walking from the hotel to the volcano park

After spending my first day reaching the volcano district, I decided to visit the volcano park the next morning.

The viewpoint was only about thirty minutes from my hotel, so I walked instead of arranging transportation.

To my surprise, many local residents were already outside exercising.

The route was simple. I only needed to follow a paved road uphill until reaching the entrance to the volcano park.

At the entrance, I could already see how carefully planned the roads were throughout the area.

Exploring the volcano park

After entering the park and climbing a few stairways, I reached a large open area.

Many locals were jogging, walking, and exercising there.

I arrived at around 6:30 a.m., yet people were already active throughout the park.

If the weather is good, arriving earlier for sunrise may be worthwhile.

Further uphill, I found another entrance leading to a maintained walking path.

As I continued climbing, volcanoes gradually appeared in the distance.

Interestingly, the closest volcano did not immediately look like a volcano because a temple had been built on top of it.

The higher I climbed, the more volcanoes became visible.

The volcano viewpoint experience

The viewpoint was the highlight of my visit.

Looking across a flat plain with multiple extinct volcanoes rising from the landscape felt very different from mountain scenery I had experienced elsewhere.

Unfortunately, visibility was reduced by air pollution on the day of my visit.

Even so, the volcanoes were impressive.

In my opinion, photographs do not fully capture how large they appear in person.

Because the surrounding terrain is mostly flat, nothing blocks the view.

This makes the volcanoes feel even larger and more dramatic.

Walking to a nearby volcano

After spending time at the viewpoint, I noticed another volcano nearby and decided to walk toward it.

The route was easy because I could see the volcano the entire time.

Most of the walk followed a quiet paved road.

During more than an hour of walking, I saw only a few vehicles.

Several cyclists passed by, and some people appeared surprised that I was walking such a long distance.

The walk itself was pleasant, but I personally found the distant volcano views from the viewpoint more impressive than the volcano up close.

If I returned, I would spend more time enjoying the viewpoint instead.

Sun protection is essential

The most important item I brought was an umbrella.

There was almost no shade anywhere along the route.

During summer, the sun can be intense because there are very few trees.

I strongly recommend bringing:

  • Water
  • Sunscreen
  • A hat or umbrella

Without sun protection, the walk could become uncomfortable very quickly.

Final thoughts

I think the volcano viewpoint is one of the best things to do if you are staying near the Datong Volcano district.

The route is easy, the scenery is unusual, and the landscape feels very different from typical hiking destinations.

Although many visitors travel to the main geopark by taxi, I found that the viewpoint near the volcano district provided more than enough scenery for a memorable visit.

For independent travelers, it was one of the most enjoyable parts of my Datong trip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you walk to the Datong Volcano Viewpoint from nearby hotels?

Yes. During my visit, the viewpoint was about thirty minutes on foot from Datong Kaijie Inn (Volcano Park Branch).

Is the walk difficult?

No. The route mainly followed paved roads, stairs, and maintained paths. I considered it an easy walk rather than a serious hike.

Do you need a taxi to reach the viewpoint?

No. The viewpoint can be reached on foot if you are staying near the volcano district. However, some of the more distant volcanoes may require transportation.

What is the most important thing to bring?

An umbrella or other sun protection. There was very little shade along the route during my visit.

Is the viewpoint worth visiting if visibility is not perfect?

Yes. Even with air pollution affecting visibility, I still found the volcano landscape impressive and worthwhile.

Should I walk to the nearby volcano after visiting the viewpoint?

You can, but personally I found the distant views from the viewpoint more impressive than the nearby volcano itself.
