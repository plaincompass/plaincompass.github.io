Zhujiajiao: A Quiet Water Village Escape Near Shanghai

Why I Visited

I went to Zhujak House because it’s the closest traditional water village to Shanghai’s city center. The trip was part of a broader visit to Shanghai, and I wanted a quick escape to a quieter, historic setting.

What Stood Out

The village itself was quiet and beautiful. A swing‑shaped bench under a purple acacia tree became my favorite spot—sitting there while listening to the flowing water felt peaceful. Most locations were exotic photo spots, and the arch bridge offered a unique perspective for shots. The weather was clear and pleasant, not humid, which made strolling around enjoyable. I found the crowd lighter than I expected; navigating the narrow streets was easy.

Practical Notes

  • Transport – I used a pickup service from Shanghai Hongqiao Airport directly to Zhujak House for 140 yuan. The next day I took the subway into Shanghai for 4 yuan per person. The walk from the hotel to Zhujak House takes about 10 minutes.
  • Duration – I stayed one night, two days, visiting from 11 pm the previous night to 11 am the next day.
  • Accessibility – The arch bridge inside Zhujak House is a bit steep; other areas are mostly flat, so walking was comfortable.
  • Language – Most merchants do not speak English, but a translation app works well.
  • Payment – Alipay or WeChat Pay is required; no cash accepted.
  • Crowd – The village was not crowded, especially in the morning.
  • Waiting time – There was no waiting time at most places.
  • Bug issue – The accommodation had cockroaches, which was disappointing. As a water village, some bugs are unavoidable, and the humidity is generally high.
  • What to bring – A sunshade is useful, and having Alipay/WeChat Pay ready is essential.

Final Thoughts

Overall, Zhujak House felt like a quiet water village with a pleasant climate. Strolling leisurely and taking photos in front of beautiful spots made the entire village feel like a photo zone. I highly recommend it for those who want to experience an old Chinese village floating on water.
