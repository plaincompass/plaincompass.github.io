Forbidden City in June: Crowded Heat, North Gate Garden, and Water Reflections

I braved a 30-minute wait and sweltering crowds at the Forbidden City, finding a quiet garden near the north gate and peaceful waterfront reflections.

Beijing, China

In June 2025, I arrived at the Forbidden City around 9:00 AM after taking the subway. The sheer size of the palace complex surprised me. It is enormous and has very few trees, which gives it a stark, open feel. One highlight was the Imperial Garden near the north gate, a space that was once used by members of the imperial family and palace women for leisure and walks. Outside the Forbidden City, the palace walls reflected beautifully in the surrounding moat, creating an unexpectedly peaceful scene and becoming one of my favorite views of the day.

The visit was extremely crowded, with about a 30-minute wait at the entrance. Even a short visit requires quite a bit of walking. Restrooms were difficult to find inside, and the June heat was exhausting, so bringing a hat and water is highly recommended. I used Alipay and WeChat Pay throughout my trip and reserved my ticket in advance through the Forbidden City mini-program on WeChat, paying 60 yuan.

At the entrance, my bag was inspected and a sunscreen spray I was carrying was flagged by security. Although there was a language barrier, I was able to communicate using a translation app and disposed of the spray on the spot. I entered through the East Gate, which allowed me to start from a less crowded part of the complex. If you plan to enter through Tiananmen, keep in mind that a separate reservation is required through the Tiananmen mini-program on WeChat, even though admission is free.

Why I Visited

I came to Beijing primarily to see the Forbidden City and the Great Wall, so I chose the Forbidden City as my first stop. Getting there by public transportation was easier than I expected, and finding the entrance was straightforward.

What Stood Out

The sheer size of the Forbidden City surprised me. I had imagined a much smaller palace, but the grounds are vast and stretch much farther than they appear in photos. The lack of trees creates a grand yet somewhat stark atmosphere.

One of the highlights was the Imperial Garden near the north gate. Compared with the vast open courtyards throughout most of the Forbidden City, the garden felt much more intimate and relaxing. Historically, it was used by members of the imperial family and palace women as a place for leisure and walks. The combination of traditional landscaping, rock formations, and old trees made it one of the most memorable parts of my visit.

The crowds inside were intense. Taking photos without other visitors in the frame was nearly impossible. Outside the complex, however, the atmosphere was much calmer. Along the moat surrounding the Forbidden City, the walls of the palace reflected beautifully on the water. The area felt surprisingly peaceful, almost like a local park where residents come to walk or run, and it made for a great photo backdrop.

Although Beijing is often associated with air pollution, I was fortunate to visit on a clear day with blue skies and excellent visibility.

Practical Notes

  • Travel & Access: Take the subway and walk from the nearest station to the entrance.
  • Waiting Time: Expect about a 30-minute wait at the entrance. It may be longer if entering through Tiananmen.
  • Crowds: Very crowded throughout the day.
  • Weather: June felt extremely hot, comparable to midsummer temperatures.
  • Fitness Level: The site is mostly flat, but the combination of walking, heat, and limited shade can be tiring.
  • Language: English was not widely spoken at the entrance, so a translation app was helpful.
  • Payment: Alipay and WeChat Pay were accepted everywhere I visited.
  • Tickets: Reserve your ticket in advance through the Forbidden City mini-program on WeChat. Admission cost 60 yuan. Tiananmen Square requires a separate reservation, although entry is free.
  • Restrooms: Available, but not always easy to locate.
  • Time Needed: I spent about two hours inside the complex, and even a short visit involves a considerable amount of walking.
  • What to Bring: A hat, water, sunscreen, and comfortable walking shoes.
  • Who It's For: Visitors interested in Chinese history, architecture, and large-scale sightseeing.
  • Who It's Not For: People who have difficulty walking long distances or handling hot weather.

Final Thoughts

The Forbidden City is an enormous historical site that can feel overwhelming because of both its size and the number of visitors. Planning ahead is important, especially when it comes to ticket reservations. If possible, visit early in the day and prepare for the heat.

While the crowds and long walks were challenging, the architecture, historical atmosphere, and the Imperial Garden near the north gate made the experience worthwhile. The peaceful views along the moat outside the Forbidden City were an unexpected highlight and provided some of my favorite photo opportunities in Beijing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get to the Forbidden City from downtown Beijing?

Take the subway and walk from the nearest station to the entrance of the Forbidden City.

What is the expected wait time at the entrance?

The wait was about 30 minutes when I visited, although it may be longer during peak periods or when entering through Tiananmen.

How long should I plan to spend at the Forbidden City?

Allow at least two hours inside the complex, plus additional time for walking around the site.

What payment methods can I use at the Forbidden City?

Alipay and WeChat Pay were widely accepted during my visit.

Are there restrooms available inside the Forbidden City?

Yes, but they can be difficult to find, so it is a good idea to use one before entering if possible.

What should I bring to make my visit more comfortable?

Bring a hat, water, sunscreen, and comfortable walking shoes, especially during the summer months.
