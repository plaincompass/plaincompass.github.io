Submerged Great Wall and Riverside Serenity at Huanghua Qingcheng

Rode a boat to a submerged section of the Great Wall, took a lift train up the mountain, and enjoyed quiet riverside views and a peaceful walk.

Huanghua Qingcheng, China

I had heard the Great Wall at Huanghua Qingcheng is partially submerged in water, so I went there in June 2025. I arrived at 10 a.m. and left at 3 p.m., a five‑hour visit. I walked ten minutes to the dock, rode a five‑minute boat (included in the day tour), and then took a lift train up the mountain—no waiting time and no public transport to the site.

The walls were small and hard to spot, but the river backdrop made the scene feel surreal and peaceful. It was quiet and the weather was good. Because the stairs are steep and the sun strong, I recommend taking the lift or cable car and resting a bit inside the fortress to avoid heat. Bring an umbrella or hat and water; the guide spoke English, and payment was via Alipay or WeChat Pay.

I was able to buy cable car tickets in advance from the guide on the day tour I used. I originally purchased it from Klook, but the product is no longer available. Instead, please refer to the trip.com product link. https://kr.trip.com/things-to-do/detail/108995524/

Why I Visited

I had heard that the Great Wall at Huanghua Qingcheng is partially submerged in water, a feature that was unlike anything I had seen before. That curiosity pulled me to this less‑known section of the Wall in June 2025.

What Stood Out

After coming down from the Great Wall, there is a riverside where I spread a mat and let the silence settle around me. The view from the mat showed the Great Wall running along the mountain ridge, with a quietly flowing river below creating a peaceful scene. The walls on the ridge were too small in photos to capture easily, but once I entered the Wall, I could frame the river as a background—an image that felt truly exotic. The weather was very good, and the site was not crowded.

Practical Notes

  • Arrival & Stay: I arrived at 10 a.m. and left at 3 p.m., a five‑hour visit.
  • Transport: From Beijing it took about 2 ½ hours to get to the parking lot by tourist bus. I walked 10 minutes to the dock, then rode a 5‑minute boat to Huanghua Qingcheng. The boat ticket was included in the day tour. From the dock, I rode a lift train up the mountain. There was no waiting time, and no public transportation to the site.
  • Access & Difficulty: The Great Wall is a military facility and not very tourist‑friendly. Even a few steps were hard, so I used the lift train. The overall difficulty level was 3.
  • Fitness: If you have done Himalayan climbing or climbed a 1,000 m mountain without rest, you can climb directly without a lift or cable car. Otherwise, please make sure to purchase round-trip cable car or lift tickets.
  • Language & Payment: The guide spoke English, so there was no language barrier. Payment was made via Alipay or WeChat Pay.
  • What to Bring: An umbrella or hat and water.
  • Crowd: There were relatively few people.

Final Thoughts

Walking around quietly was entirely possible. The view of the Great Wall with water as a backdrop felt exotic and different from the usual photos. Because the stairs are steep and the sun strong, I recommend taking the cable car or lift. Resting a bit inside the fortress helps avoid the heat. Rather than trying to see the whole Wall, I suggest taking a few good photos of a section, then heading down to enjoy the peaceful riverside.
