Summer Palace in Beijing: Views from the Tower of Buddhist Incense

I spent two hours exploring the Summer Palace in Beijing, enjoying panoramic views from the Tower of Buddhist Incense and the park's peaceful atmosphere.

Summer Palace, Beijing, China

Public transport access

I decided to visit the Summer Palace after hearing it was a Qing dynasty imperial garden closely associated with Empress Dowager Cixi and one of the most beautiful places in Beijing. The journey was straightforward: I took the subway from my hotel to Beigongmen Station, walked to the entrance, and spent about an hour getting there. I arrived at 10:00 AM and paid the admission fee (30 yuan) via Alipay.

The garden itself is a spacious and serene setting with a large artificial lake, floating cruise boats, a winding stream, and the Tower of Buddhist Incense overlooking the grounds. The clear weather made the colors especially vivid, and although the park was busy, it never felt overcrowded. Exploring the grounds took time, and reaching the Tower of Buddhist Incense required climbing quite a few steps. Even so, the overall experience felt peaceful and relaxing, providing a welcome contrast to the bustle of Tiananmen Square the previous day.

Why I Visited

I had heard that the Summer Palace was a Qing dynasty imperial garden closely associated with Empress Dowager Cixi and the Chinese imperial court. That historical connection drew me in, and I wanted to see for myself the garden that many people consider one of the most beautiful places in Beijing.

What Stood Out

The scenery and architecture were impressive from every angle. A vast artificial lake dominates the center of the grounds, and the sight of cruise boats gliding across the water added a gentle rhythm to the landscape.

The highlight of my visit was the view from the Tower of Buddhist Incense. Looking across the entire park from above was breathtaking. The small streams winding through the grounds were another memorable detail. Even with many visitors present, the palace grounds felt spacious enough for a leisurely walk. The clear weather made the colors of the gardens, buildings, and lake even more vibrant.

Practical Notes

  • Transport: I took the subway from my hotel to Beigongmen Station and walked to the entrance. The entire journey took about one hour.
  • Arrival Time: I arrived at 10:00 AM and spent approximately two hours exploring.
  • Admission Fee: I paid the 30-yuan admission fee via Alipay.
  • Crowds: The park was busy but never felt crowded.
  • Physical Effort: The grounds are extensive, so expect a fair amount of walking. Reaching the Tower of Buddhist Incense requires climbing several flights of steps.
  • Weather & Comfort: The weather was clear, and I was glad to have brought water and an umbrella.
  • Waiting Time: There was no waiting time upon entry.

Final Thoughts

The Summer Palace felt calm and leisurely, forming a perfect contrast to the crowds at Tiananmen Square the day before. The combination of traditional Chinese architecture and natural scenery was stunning, and the scale of the artificial lake left a lasting impression. Overall, it fully lived up to its reputation as one of Beijing's most beautiful places.
